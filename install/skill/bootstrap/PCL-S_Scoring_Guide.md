# PCL-S (DSM-IV) — Scoring & Interpretation Guide

A reference for the 17-item PCL-S used in the CPT-SA manual. The manual instructs administering the PCL at the start of the even-numbered sessions (2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, and 14) but does not explain how to score or interpret it — this document fills that gap.

## The instrument

Each of the 17 items is rated on a five-point scale for how much the client has been bothered by that problem:

| Rating | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Label | Not at all | A little bit | Moderately | Quite a bit | Extremely |

The **total score** is the sum of all 17 items and ranges from **17 to 85**.

## Method 1 — Total score against a cutoff

Add the 17 items and compare to a cutoff for a probable PTSD diagnosis.

- **50** — the classic cutoff (Weathers et al., 1993), calibrated on combat/veteran samples. Sensitivity ~.78–.82, specificity ~.83–.86.
- **~44** — better suited to civilian/specialty samples (e.g., 44 gave sensitivity .94, specificity .86 in motor-vehicle-accident survivors).

**For a childhood-sexual-abuse population** (as in CPT-SA), a cutoff in the **mid-40s** is generally more appropriate than 50, since 50 was validated on combat veterans.

## Method 2 — DSM symptom-cluster method

Maps onto the three symptom clusters described in Session 1. An item counts as an endorsed symptom if rated **3 or higher** (Moderately, Quite a bit, or Extremely).

| Criterion | Cluster | Items | Threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| B | Reexperiencing | 1–5 | at least **1** item ≥ 3 |
| C | Avoidance / numbing | 6–12 | at least **3** items ≥ 3 |
| D | Hyperarousal | 13–17 | at least **2** items ≥ 3 |

A probable diagnosis requires meeting all three thresholds. Some combine the methods — require the cluster pattern **and** a total above cutoff.

## Tracking change over time

This is the main reason for administering the PCL repeatedly. Compare each administration's total to the prior one(s):

- **5-point drop** — minimum considered **reliable change** (beyond measurement noise / not due to chance).
- **10-point drop** — threshold for **clinically meaningful** improvement.

Example: a client moving from 62 to 51 over two weeks shows an 11-point reduction — genuine, meaningful progress rather than random fluctuation.

## Important caveat for this manual

The standard PCL asks about the **past month**, but the weekly version in the CPT-SA manual has been adapted to **past-week** recall so it can be repeated each session. The scoring math and change thresholds still apply, but the cutoffs were validated on the monthly version — so treat the weekly totals more as a **within-person trend line** than as a strict diagnostic score.
