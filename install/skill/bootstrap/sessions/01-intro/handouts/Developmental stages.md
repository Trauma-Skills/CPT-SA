# Developmental stages

The following is a list of the stages of development children move through as they grow. Beneath each age range is a list of abilities a typical child of that age will develop. It's also normal for some children to reach these earlier or later. As you read, try to remember yourself at that age. The list represents both what you may have accomplished at a given age, and the developmental tasks that may have been affected by the abuse.

## Infant (0–24 months)

**0–6 months**

- vision not yet well developed; hearing acute
- learns to reach, grab, and hold objects
- may begin crawling; can stand without support
- if basic physical needs are met, the ability to trust begins to develop

**6–12 months**

- can walk holding a hand; learns to crawl; by 12 months can walk unassisted
- develops specific wants (pursues a favourite toy)
- if the child experiences loving touch, the ability to love begins to develop
- children begin to explore their own bodies

**12–18 months**

- discovery and exploration of the environment
- walking
- identity as male or female begins to develop

**18–24 months**

- develops the ability to anticipate and solve simple problems
- learns by imitation and modelling
- learns to be more self-sufficient
- if parents support the child's budding independence, confidence develops

## Early childhood (2–6 years)

- by age 5, the typical child weighs around 40 lb and is about 3'7"
- fine motor skills develop (colouring, writing, drawing)
- by 5, most children can ride a tricycle, climb a ladder, pump a swing, run well, kick a ball
- the ability to represent thoughts through words or images begins to develop
- the child believes other people and objects think and act as they do
- becomes curious about the human body, including the sexual parts of the body
- language and communication skills improve
- learns to pretend and engage in fantasy play
- begins to play *with* others rather than alongside them
- needs warm, loving parents who set good limits and establish clear, consistent rules with consequences

## Childhood (7–11 years)

- by age 10, the typical child weighs around 70 lb and is about 4'6"
- motor skills developed
- memory, reasoning, and learning are increasing
- good — or poor — self-esteem continues to develop as children master or fail new tasks
- the ability to recognise individual differences and take others' points of view develops

## Adolescence (10–16 years)

- developing a personal identity is the primary task of this stage
- puberty begins (physical changes, onset of sexual desire)
- the ability to think abstractly develops
- the questioning of traditional customs, laws, and values begins
- the opinions of peers, and peer relationships, become extremely important
