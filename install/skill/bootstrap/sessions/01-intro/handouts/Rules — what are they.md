# Rules — what are they?

Throughout the rest of this course we'll be talking about "rules" and helping you identify your own. Rules are conflicting beliefs, or strong beliefs, that create unpleasant emotions or problems for you. They can form in a couple of different ways.

## 1. A rule can form from a conflict between a belief and the abuse

| Prior belief | The abuse |
|---|---|
| Good things happen to good people. | You were abused. |

**What can happen**

- If you can't change the belief to accept what happened (that bad things *can* happen to good people), you may find yourself asking "Was it really abuse?"
- From there you might make sense of it by saying things like: "I must have misinterpreted what happened… I didn't make myself clear… I must be crazy… I must have done something to mislead them… I am bad."
- If you get stuck here, it can take some time before you're able to let your feelings about the abuse come out.

**The goal**

- To help you change the prior belief to "Bad things can happen to good people." Once you can do that, you're able to name the event as abuse — to accept that it happened — and move forward from there.

## 2. A rule can form when a negative belief is confirmed or reinforced by the abuse

| Prior belief | The abuse |
|---|---|
| Sex is bad. | You were sexually abused. |

**What can happen**

- If you see the abuse as further proof that "sex is bad," you may come to believe it very strongly — and it can become a rule.
- Stuck here, you may have strong emotional reactions that interfere with your ability or desire to have sexual relationships. That can feel "safe," but unless it's how you want to live the rest of your life, it deserves some attention.

**The goal**

- To help you soften beliefs so they're less extreme — for example, "Sexual abuse is bad, but sexual intimacy is not bad."
