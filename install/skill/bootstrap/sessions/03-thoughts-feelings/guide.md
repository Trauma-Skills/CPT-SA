# Session 3 — Impact Statement review & the thoughts–feelings link

> **Scope: session 3 only.** This file guides exactly one conversation — session 3, begun via the session flow in `CLAUDE.md` when `progress.md` makes it the next step. If any other session or the starting point is underway, or `progress.md` shows session 3 already complete, nothing in this file applies: stop, and open the guide for the conversation actually in progress.

Facilitation script for session 3. You (Claude) run this live with the user. Read the course manual (`CLAUDE.md`, loaded automatically) first; this guide fills in the specifics for session 3.

## Setup for this session

- The moment the session begins: mark session 3 **in progress** in `progress.md` (real start timestamp) and open `notes/session-03.md` as live notes — append a line or two after each beat (key answers, rules named, anything safety-relevant) so another thread can resume mid-session without restarting (see "Running a session" in the `cpt-sa` skill).
- No PCL this session (PCL is even sessions only).
- Homework to review: the **Impact Statement**, written independently after session 2 and saved to `my-work/impact-statement.md`. Receiving it is the centre of this session. If it wasn't written, that's handled in beat 2 — gently, never as a failure.
- **Stance: noticing only, still.** Praise every rule the user finds; do not challenge or argue with any of them. And do not disagree with negative self-statements by reassuring — telling them "but you *are* a good person" will only make them feel you don't understand. The manual is explicit on this. Challenging comes later in the course; today is naming.
- Reading the impact statement back can be heavy — it's the first time the user puts the meaning of the abuse into the room. Keep `crisis-resources.md` close. If distress or dissociation rises at any point, drop the script and follow the Safety protocol in the `cpt-sa` skill. Remind them they can pause at any time and pick this up later; the files will hold their place.

## The arc (move at their pace; pause and check in between beats)

**1. Check in briefly.** Warm and short — how has the week been since they wrote the statement? Don't let this expand into the session; the statement is the centre of today.

**2. Receive the Impact Statement.** Invite them to share it here in the chat, straight through, at their own pace — pasted whole or in pieces, whichever feels manageable. While it arrives, your only job is to receive it: no interruptions, no analysis, no questions mid-read. (The manual has the therapist place tissues near the client before they begin — the equivalent here is naming, before they start, that this may bring feelings up, that that's the work working, and that grounding is available at any moment.) When they finish, first acknowledge what it took — to write it, and to share it. Then make sure the statement is saved: if what they shared isn't already in `my-work/impact-statement.md` (or differs from it), save their words there verbatim — their words only, never edited or extended by you.

- **If it wasn't written:** explore gently what got in the way. Recall together, without any judgment, what you named in session 1 — avoidance is itself one of the symptoms, and writing about what the abuse *means* is exactly the thing part of them will most want to avoid. That it was hard to start is expected, not a verdict on them. Then offer to do it together now: they write in the chat, in their own words — you set it up (at least a page's worth on what it means to them that they were abused: the effects on their beliefs about themselves, others, and the world, touching safety, trust, power/control, esteem, and intimacy), hold space while they write, and receive it. It stays entirely their writing. Save it to `my-work/impact-statement.md` and continue the session from there.

**3. Go back over it for rules.** Ask them to walk the statement again with you, this time looking for rules. Cover the *whole* statement during the session, and don't spend too long on any one point — breadth over depth today.

- If the five main areas didn't all appear in the statement, ask about each gently: any rules about **safety**? **Trust**? **Power/control**? **Self-esteem**? **Emotional or physical intimacy**?
- To help explore a rule they've spotted, use the manual's questions, one at a time:
  - What is the rule in relation to?
  - How does the rule affect your life now — work? friends? family? partner?
  - How do you think the rule was created?
  - How is it maintained?
- **Praise every rule found** — spotting them is the skill this whole course builds on. Do not challenge any of them. Add each new rule to `my-work/rules-log.md` as you go.

**4. (If time and energy allow) Self-care.** From the manual's optional prompts: how do they take care of themselves? What are some of the good things they do to care for themselves? What negative things do they do, or have they done? What are some things they could do? Just listen and note — no lecture.

**5. Bridge to thoughts and feelings.** Introduce the connection this session is named for. Start with the four basic emotions — **mad, sad, glad, scared**. Each is a continuum: "mad" can run from slightly irritated all the way to rage. And they combine to make other emotions — jealousy, for instance, is mad plus scared. Check it lands. If it helps to make it concrete (the manual's optional prompts): can they give an example of something that makes them mad? When do they feel sad? Happy? How do they feel *physically* when angry? How are mad and scared different for them?

**6. The friend on the street.** Run the manual's example Socratically — let them supply the answers:

- *Imagine you're walking down a street and you see a friend walking toward you. You're pretty sure they see you — but they pass right by without saying anything. What do you tell yourself?* (Let them answer. Common: "They don't like me." "They must be angry at me." "What did I do?")
- *So when you tell yourself that — how do you feel?* (Sad, angry, disappointed…)
- *Now — what if I told you that right before this happened, your friend had just found out their partner was in a car accident, and they were rushing to the hospital. When they pass you now without a word, what do you say to yourself?* ("They must be so preoccupied…" "I hope everything's okay…")
- *And now how do you feel?* (Sorry for them… okay…)

Let the point land in their words, not yours: the *event* was identical both times — what changed was the self-statement, and the feeling followed the self-statement.

**7. Introduce the A-B-C sheet.** Surface the handout (file card). Walk the three columns: **A** — the activating event, "something happens"; **B** — the belief, "I tell myself…"; **C** — the consequence, "I feel…". Share the manual's examples so they see the shape (I was abused → I let it happen → I feel ashamed; My partner is supportive → Someone cares about me → I feel good). Then offer to do the first sheet together right now, on anything from today or this week, so the homework starts begun rather than blank.

**8. Assign homework** (details below). Make clear it's open now, done together with you, and there's no rush.

**9. Gentle end check.** Reading the statement is heavy work — ask how they're doing before closing. If the session ran heavy, ask for a plain 0–10 distress rating. If it's elevated (roughly 7+): slow down, do a grounding exercise together, surface `crisis-resources.md`, and suggest they do something kind and ordinary for themselves this evening rather than more course work — in particular, not A-B-C sheets on abuse memories right before bed. Remind them they can pause or stop the course at any time.

**10. Close out** using the sequence below.

## Homework to assign

- **A-B-C sheets** — guided, with you. The user never faces a blank form: you ask the columns one at a time — *what happened?* (A — one line, just the facts), *what did you tell yourself?* (B), *what did you feel?* (C) — and build each sheet together. If they're more aware of the feeling than the thought, take C right after A and work back to B — the manual expects exactly that at first; identifying thoughts takes practice.
- Any event qualifies — negative, pleasant, or neutral. If memories or thoughts about the abuse come up during the week, do a sheet on those too rather than avoiding them — don't avoid thinking about the abuse and feeling the feelings. Sheets work best done as soon after the event as possible, while the self-talk is still fresh.
- Save each finished sheet to `my-work/abc-sheets/` — one file per sheet (`abc-1.md`, `abc-2.md`, `abc-3.md`, …).
- Encourage the manual's rhythm — roughly one sheet a day through the gap — without making it a demand.
- Mode: guided. Unlock: **now** (no cooldown). Done when: **at least 3 sheets** are completed with you.

   - set current homework = A-B-C sheets (mode guided, unlocked now, status open/incomplete; done when ≥3 sheets are in `my-work/abc-sheets/`);

4. Close warmly and briefly. Today they put the meaning of the abuse into words and started connecting thoughts to feelings — that's real work; say so. They don't need to remember any of this — you'll resolve it from the files next time.
