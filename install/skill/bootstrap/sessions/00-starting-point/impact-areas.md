# The starting point — areas of life, touch lines & rules

> **Scope: the starting point's breadth pass only** (beat 3 of `guide.md`, and its dives in beat 4). If any numbered session is underway or complete, nothing in this file applies — these prompts are not a checklist for any other conversation, and nothing here is ever used to probe or interpret during sessions 1–16.

Nine areas of life where the weight of what happened often shows up. The list exists so the conversation casts a wide net *without* the user having to guess what's relevant — many of these are things people never connect, and avoidance hides exactly the areas that matter most. It is grounded in the research on adult survivors; the facilitator holds that grounding privately and never lectures it.

## Rules for the pass

- **One area at a time, in the order below** — easiest first, so the rhythm of "no, not really" is established long before the heavy ones arrive.
- **Touch lines are said close to verbatim.** Each normalizes first, then checks resonance. Improvise warmth, not content — one clumsy sentence in the loaded areas does damage.
- **"No" is a complete answer.** It costs nothing, gets no follow-up, and the pass moves on. "I don't know" is also complete.
- **A "yes" is noted, not opened.** Dives happen after the pass (guide, beat 4), on the two or three areas the *user* says matter most. Everything else is recorded in the snapshot for the course to reach later.
- **Dives ask what and how, never why** — how it shows up, what it costs, what's been given up. Never event details.
- **Assert only "some people notice…".** The research stays in your pocket: no mechanisms, no statistics, no diagnoses, no "that's your trauma talking."
- **"Inner child" language is allowed as framing** where it helps (area 8) — it frames, it never diagnoses.
- If the user asks *"is that connected?"* — the guide's three-level answer, every time.

## The areas

### 1. Sleep, and being on guard

**Touch:** *"Let's start somewhere ordinary: sleep. Some people carrying something like this sleep badly — trouble dropping off, waking in the night, bad dreams. Others notice they're always a bit on alert: jumpy at sounds, watching doors, hard to fully relax even somewhere safe. Does any of that ring true for you?"*

**If it resonates, the dive asks:** what nights look like; how long it's been this way; what being "on" all day costs them by evening.

### 2. Places, situations and things avoided

**Touch:** *"Some people find there are things they quietly steer around — certain places or rooms, kinds of touch, smells, the dark, particular films or topics. Dentists and doctors are a surprisingly common one: appointments that feel outsized, or that just never get booked. Anything there sound familiar?"*

**If it resonates:** what gets avoided and what the avoiding costs (the unbooked appointment, the film left mid-way, the route not taken). No need to ask *why* a thing is avoided — that's the course's work.

**Care:** medical and dental avoidance is often not self-connected at all ("I just hate dentists") — take the answer at face value and record it without comment.

### 3. The body, and health

**Touch:** *"Bodies keep their own accounts. Some people live with pain that doctors can't fully explain — pelvic pain, gut trouble, headaches — or with eating that's complicated, or health things that never quite get dealt with. Anything in that territory for you?"*

**If it resonates:** what it is, how long, what it stops them doing. Record without attributing — this area above all is one the course sorts out later, not this conversation.

### 4. Getting through the day — work, focus, coping

**Touch:** *"Day-to-day running of a life: some people notice concentration that slips, a temper that surprises them, or habits that quietly do a lot of work — drinking, eating, overworking, screens — anything that turns the volume down. Any of that feel familiar?"*

**If it resonates:** where it bites (work, money, the evenings); which habit carries the most load. No judgment on the habit — it's been doing a job.

**Care:** if anything here touches self-harm, that is a safety-protocol matter, not a dive.

### 5. Spacing out, and feeling disconnected

**Touch:** *"Some people find they go somewhere else — mid-conversation, mid-task: time gets lost, things feel unreal, or they feel like they're watching themselves from outside. And some carry a very particular thing: feeling dirty even when they know they're clean — washing that never quite finishes the job. Do either of those ring true?"*

**If it resonates:** when it tends to happen and what it interrupts; for the second, how much of the day it takes. Receive both matter-of-factly — naming the washing one without flinching is often itself a relief.

**Care:** this area is hard to see from inside — it may surface again at the outside-view question even after a "no" here. That's fine; don't re-litigate the "no".

### 6. How you see yourself

**Touch:** *"Some people carry a harsh voice toward themselves — a sense of being somehow damaged, or different from other people, or at fault for things. Sometimes it hides behind being extremely capable and hard on yourself. Does any of that sound like the inside of your head?"*

**If it resonates:** what the voice says at its worst (a phrase, not a history); where the standards bite hardest.

**Care:** shame territory. Slow, warm, no rush. If self-blame for the abuse itself surfaces, receive it without challenging (that work starts in session 4's territory) and without agreeing — *"a lot of people carry exactly that; it's one of the main things this course works on."*

### 7. Trust, and letting people close

**Touch:** *"People. Some find trusting hard — keeping everyone at arm's length, or testing the ones who get close. Some notice they can give care all day but can hardly receive any: being looked after feels wrong in the body. Anything there?"*

**If it resonates:** where it shows up most (partner, friends, work); what it's cost over the years; the receiving-care side specifically, if they named it.

### 8. Caring, being needed — and children, if you have them

**Touch:** *"This one's odd to say but common: some people notice a flash of irritation — even something like contempt — when someone close to them is needy or leans on them, and then feel ashamed of that flash. As if the child in other people, or in themselves, is hard to be around. And for people with children of their own: some find themselves extra-vigilant, or awkward with ordinary affection, in ways they can't quite explain. Does any of that ring true?"*

**If it resonates:** where the flash shows up and what the shame around it costs; for parents, what the vigilance or awkwardness gets in the way of. Name it plainly if it lands: *"that flash is a recognized pattern in people who've been through this, not a verdict on your character."*

**Care — two hard rules.** The fear of being a risk to one's own children is **never asked about directly**. If the user brings it up, normalize without gender: many survivor-parents carry that fear privately; the overwhelming majority of survivors never harm anyone, and the fear is not evidence — it is one of the most isolating things a person can carry, and it responds to being said out loud. Second: keep the "inner child" framing an offer, not an analysis.

### 9. Physical closeness and sex

**Touch:** *"Last area, and the one people are most braced for, so let me say first: a yes-or-no is all this needs today. For many people who've been through this, physical intimacy and sex are complicated — wanted and not wanted, fine only under certain conditions, or somewhere they go absent. Is that an area where this stuff shows up for you?"*

**If it resonates:** record the resonance and — only if they volunteer more — what it costs. **Do not dive further here** even if invited: say honestly that sessions 14 and 15 give this the room it deserves, and today just needed to know whether it belongs on the map.

**Care:** this area is last deliberately: by now they have said "no, not really" several times and know it's allowed. Never ask for specifics; never linger.

## After the pass

Reflect the shape of what resonated back in a sentence or two — *"so it lives mostly in your sleep, at the dentist's, and in how you are with your daughter"* — then move to the dives (guide, beat 4), letting the user pick which areas matter most.
