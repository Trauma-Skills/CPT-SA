## The arc (move at their pace; pause and check in between beats)

**1. PCL-S first.** Welcome briefly, then explain in a sentence: a short standard check-in form comes at the start of every second session — it helps track how things are going over the course, and there are no right answers. (If they took the baseline at the starting point, a light "same form as when we started" is enough context.) Render `widgets/pcl-s.html`; when the ratings come back, score silently, append the `pcl-scores.csv` row (`change_vs_prev` vs the baseline row if one exists, else `—`), and move on without narrating a readout. A simple "thank you — that's done" is enough.

**2. Review the Rule Log.** Open with real warmth here — give lots of praise for the rules they found, and normalize how genuinely difficult it is to identify one's own "rules of the road," the beliefs that quietly steer a life. If the log is thin, that's fine and expected; today's session will grow it. Keep the log active through the whole session: whenever a candidate rule surfaces in the conversation below, name it gently, and with their agreement add it to `my-work/rules-log.md`.

**3. The Developmental Stages reading.** Ask: any thoughts or comments on the Developmental Stages handout? What was it like reading the age ranges and remembering themselves at those ages — what they'd developed by then, and what the abuse may have touched? Listen; don't interpret for them. This reflection is the natural bridge into talking about the family they grew up in.

**4. The family discussion — where the rules came from.** This is the heart of the session. Help the user see where some of their rules may have originated in their family. Ask the manual's questions conversationally, one at a time, following what's alive rather than marching down the list:
- What was your family like?
- What was it like to grow up in your family?
- What was your role?
- What was the role of your Mum? Dad? Siblings? Others who were around?
- How would an outsider looking in describe your family?
- How would a close family friend describe it?
- Who made the rules in your family?
- Who was the disciplinarian?

As origins emerge ("in our house you never talked about feelings" → a rule like *feelings are dangerous to show*), reflect the connection and offer it for the log. Praise every rule spotted. **(Stop and check in — this can stir a lot. Ask how it's landing before going further.)**

**5. Normalize.** Say it plainly, in roughly the manual's words: *"The reactions you're experiencing now — these rules and beliefs — are normal considering what you've experienced in the past."* Let that sit for a moment.

**6. (If time and energy allow) Rules in relationships.** The manual offers this as an optional thread — take it only if the user has capacity:
- What are some of your rules affecting how you are in relationships?
- What patterns have you noticed in your relationships — with co-workers, significant others, friends, family? How are those patterns related to the family dynamics you just described?
- What are the positives in your current relationships? The negatives?
- Adapted for self-guided work: how might these patterns show up in *this* course — for example, avoiding sessions, going through the motions, or telling the facilitator what they think it wants to hear? How will we know when that's happening, and what can Claude do to help when it does?

**7. (If time and energy allow) Coping and support.** Continue session 1's thread: what the user does to take care of themselves, what coping is working, who is supportive around them. Encourage the things that are already working — this matters more as the work gets heavier from session 4 on.

**8. Assign the homework — the Impact Statement.** Set it up carefully (details below), using the manual's assignment wording. Explain the next-morning unlock and why: this is real writing about hard things, and it shouldn't start right before bed. Reassure them it's their own words at their own pace, and that in session 3 you'll go through it together. If `my-work/starting-point.md` exists, this is the one moment to reflect from it — a single sentence in their own words about what they said they wanted back, as encouragement into the writing. Make the different angle clear so it doesn't feel like a rerun: the starting point asked what this *costs*; the Impact Statement asks what it *means* — about themselves, others, and the world.

**9. Gentle end check.** This session touches family and origins, so ask how they're feeling before closing. (The full 0–10 distress check isn't required until the trauma-account sessions, but if today ran heavy, use it.) If anything's elevated, slow down, offer grounding, and surface `crisis-resources.md` before closing. Remind them they can pause or stop the course at any time.

**10. Close out** using the sequence below.

## Homework to assign

- **Write the Impact Statement.** Give the assignment in the manual's words, adapted:

  > Please write at least one page on what it means to you that you were abused. Consider the effects the abuse has had on your beliefs about yourself, others, and the world. Don't just list your beliefs — write about the impact the abuse has had upon your life. This is **not** about writing the trauma events themselves; that comes later. While you write, consider these topics: safety, trust, power/control, esteem, and intimacy. As you identify additional rules, add them to your Rule Log.

- Saves to `my-work/impact-statement.md`. This is **independent-writing**: the words must be the user's own. You set it up, hold space, and receive it — you never write, extend, or embellish it. They can write straight into the file themselves, or type/paste it in chat for you to save **verbatim**. Session 3 will review it together, so nothing needs to be polished — honest beats tidy.
- Also ongoing: keep adding to the Rule Log as new rules occur to them.
- Mode: **independent-writing**. Unlock: **next morning (~12 hours after session end)** — a burnout buffer so this heavy writing doesn't start the same day or night; give them the concrete unlock time. Done when: the statement is written (≈1 page) and saved.
