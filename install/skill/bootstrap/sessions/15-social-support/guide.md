## The arc (move at their pace; pause and check in between beats)

**1. Settle in.** Greet them warmly; no PCL today. Name the shape of the session: finishing the intimacy work, a look at social support — the last new topic of the course — and then setting up the final piece of writing.

**2. Review the intimacy homework.** Go over their CBW(s) from `my-work/cbw/` — praise the effort, and check the alternative beliefs genuinely land rather than being "correct answers." Then work the manual's discussion questions, one at a time:
- What rules do you have about caring for and nurturing yourself?
- What are some things you can do to help nurture yourself?
- How do you build emotional intimacy with others?
- How is this related to the issues of safety, trust, power, and esteem?

**3. Triggers.** Talk through triggers and what to do with them — both in situations where they might *expect* to be triggered (e.g. physical intimacy) and ones where they might not (e.g. a news programme on television that happens to touch the topic). What has helped so far? What would they reach for in each kind of moment? Keep it practical: noticing, grounding, and the challenging skills they now have.

**4. Nice things for themselves.** Ask honestly: have they been doing nice things for themselves? If not, what rules are getting in the way? Those rules get challenged — and at this stage, invite *them* to run the challenge (the questions, the patterns, the alternative belief), stepping in only where they get stuck.

**5. Name the ending.** The next session is the last one. Ask directly: how are they doing with that? What thoughts and feelings come up about the course ending? Don't fix or reassure prematurely — listen, reflect, and note what surfaces (it often carries rules about support and self-reliance, which leads naturally into the next beat). Note anything important for `notes/session-15.md`.

**6. Introduce social support.** Surface the Social Support handout (file card) and walk its two halves conversationally:
- *Supporting yourself* — beliefs about their ability to solve their own problems and comfort themselves. How the abuse may have confirmed old doubts ("I can't handle things on my own") or shattered old confidence. The goal: identify and challenge the disruptive beliefs, then *practice* — acting as if they have the strength and tools to care for themselves.
- *Being supported by others* — beliefs about asking for and receiving support, especially if they were blamed or rejected by people they thought would be supportive. Social support isn't all-or-nothing: there are different kinds (emotional, practical, financial, advice), and no one person can supply them all.
Then make it concrete: who in their life is actually supportive, and of which kind? Who isn't? How could they build and use support after the course ends? And giving matters as well as receiving — the compliments practice runs both directions, and sharing support with people they care about is healthy, not weakness.

**7. One CBW on a support belief, together.** Pick one rule that surfaced — about handling things alone, or about asking for help — and walk a Challenging Beliefs Worksheet on it column by column, as they've been doing since session 9, with the user in the lead. Save it to `my-work/cbw/cbw-social-support-1.md`.

**8. Assign homework** (details below). Take particular care setting up the Impact Statement rewrite: read them the assignment in full, remind them where the writing goes, and ask them **not** to reread their original statement first — the point is to capture how they see it *today*, fresh; in session 16 the two statements get set side by side.

**9. Gentle end check.** Ask how they're feeling as this session closes (0–10 if that's easier). If anything's elevated (roughly 7+): slow down, offer grounding, surface `crisis-resources.md`, and advise against doing the Impact Statement writing right before bed. Remind them they can pause or stop at any time.

**10. Close out** using the sequence below.

- **Rewrite the Impact Statement** — independent-writing: this must be their own writing. The assignment, adapted from the manual: *write at least one page on what it means to you that you were abused, as you see it right now — not as you saw it when you began this course. Write about how you see yourself, others, and the world. Consider safety, trust, power/control, esteem, and intimacy as you write.* Ask them not to reread the original (`my-work/impact-statement.md`) before writing. You set it up and receive it — you never write, extend, or embellish it. Save to `my-work/impact-statement-final.md`.
- **Encouraged, not gating** (from the manual): keep doing one nice thing for themselves each day and practicing giving and receiving compliments; keep reading through their completed CBWs — the more they practice the new beliefs, the more those replace the old ones.
- Mode: **guided + independent-writing**. Unlock: **~12 hours after session end** (the next morning — never the same day or night; this is reflective, significant writing). Done when: the rewritten statement exists in `my-work/impact-statement-final.md`.
