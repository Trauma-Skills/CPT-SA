## The arc (move at their pace; pause and check in between beats)

**1. Welcome back and review homework.** Greet warmly, then go through the trust work with the manual's questions, conversationally and one at a time:
- What was it like to do the Challenging Beliefs Worksheets? Any problems?
- How was "Trust" for you? Is trust a topic that gives you problems?
- Were you able to do some sheets on it?

As you review, quietly check whether the sheets address both *self*-trust (trusting their own perceptions and judgment) and *other*-trust. If one side is missing, help them notice a rule on that side — you can work it into the sheet you'll do together later, or fold it into this week's homework.

**2. Discussion on trust.** Two Socratic questions, taken slowly:
- How do you know whom to trust?
- What are some good indicators that let you know whether or not someone is trustworthy?

Help them build their own answer — trust as gradual, evidence-based, and dimensional rather than all-or-none. Don't lecture the resolution at them; draw it out.

**3. Go over the Challenging Beliefs Worksheets.** Pick one or two of their trust sheets and walk them together. Strengthen the thin columns: is the evidence-for/against honest? Did a problematic pattern get named? Does the alternative thought actually feel believable (what's its rating)? Praise what's working before touching what isn't.

**4. (If time and energy allow) Risk-taking.** From the manual's optional topics:
- How much do you trust yourself to take risks?
- What types of risks have you taken so far?
- What risks will you have to take to get what you need from this work?
- What is stopping you from taking those risks?

**5. (If time and energy allow) Standing back.** Ask: what is it like to look at and reexamine your beliefs about yourself and about the world? Just listen — this is a check on how the whole challenging phase is sitting with them.

**6. Introduce core beliefs.** Explain plainly: a *core belief* is a belief that encompasses many of our automatic thoughts — the deeper rule the surface thoughts keep pointing back to. Show them how to dig for one from a thought they've already worked with, using the manual's two probes, one step at a time:
- "What does it mean to me if…?" (e.g. if that thought were true)
- "If A then B; and if B then… what?"

Follow the chain down two or three steps until something that feels central appears. From here on, the sheets get aimed at core beliefs, not just the automatic thoughts on top of them. Don't force it — if nothing crystallises today, naming the idea is enough.

**7. Introduce power & control.** Surface the Power & Control Issues handout (file card) and orient its two threads, inviting them to notice which resonates — as with trust, it can cut either way depending on what they believed before:
- *Self* — the belief that you can solve problems and meet challenges; when early life taught that events couldn't be controlled, *learned helplessness* can set in, showing up as numbing, passivity, hopelessness, self-destructive patterns, or outrage when things won't bend.
- *Others* — the belief that you have some power in relationships, even with powerful people; damage here shows up as passivity and submissiveness, or as needing all the control and raging when any is shared.

Ask what they notice in themselves as you go — helplessness, overcontrol, or both in different places.

**8. Start a Challenging Beliefs Worksheet on power & control together.** Take one rule that surfaced in beat 7 (or dig for it with the core-belief probes) and build the sheet in conversation, one column at a time — never as a form to fill:
- **A. Situation** — the event, thought, or belief leading to the unpleasant emotion(s).
- **B. Thought/rule** — the thought related to it; how much do they believe it, 0–100%?
- **C. Emotion(s)** — what do they feel (sad, angry, afraid…), and how strongly, 0–100%?
- **D. Challenging thoughts** — work through the Challenging Questions: evidence for? evidence against? habit or fact? all-or-none? extreme or exaggerated? out of context? source unreliable? low vs. high probability? based on feelings or facts?
- **E. Problematic patterns** — does one of their thinking patterns show up here (jumping to conclusions, exaggerating/minimizing, disregarding important aspects, oversimplifying, over-generalizing, mind reading, emotional reasoning)?
- **F. Alternative thought(s)** — what else can they say instead? How much do they believe it, 0–100%?
- **G. Re-rate the old thought** — how much do they believe the column-B thought now?
- **H. Emotion(s) now** — what do they feel now, 0–100%?

Save the finished piece to `my-work/cbw/cbw-power-control-1.md`. If energy runs out partway, save what exists and let homework finish it.

**9. Assign homework** (details below). Note any specific rules that surfaced today that deserve their own sheet this week — name them explicitly so the homework starts concrete, the way the manual's therapist hands over pre-seeded sheets.

**10. Gentle end check.** Ask how they're doing as the session closes. If the session ran heavy — core-belief work or the power/control material hit hard — make it the full 0–10 distress rating. If elevated (≈7+): slow down, ground (5-4-3-2-1), surface `crisis-resources.md`, and suggest keeping worksheet work away from right-before-bed. Remind them they can pause or stop at any time.

**11. Close out** using the sequence below.
