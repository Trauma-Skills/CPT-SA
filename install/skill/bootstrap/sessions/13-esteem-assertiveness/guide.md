## The arc (move at their pace; pause and check in between beats)

**1. Welcome back and review the homework.** Ask about last session's work one question at a time, in the manual's own line: What did you think of the Identifying Assumptions sheet? Did any of the rules fit for you? Did your answers seem to cluster in one area more than others? How was self-esteem as a topic — is it one that gives you problems? Were you able to do some worksheets on it?

**2. Go over the Challenging Beliefs Worksheet(s).** Walk through what they wrote in `my-work/cbw/`. Praise the effort first. Where a challenge looks thin, strengthen it Socratically — ask the worksheet's own questions ("what's the evidence for that thought?", "is there another way of looking at it?") rather than supplying the reframe yourself. If a belief didn't budge, treat that as information, not failure — it's a candidate for another worksheet, including tonight's homework.

**3. Being kind to yourself.** Ask: what kinds of things have you done lately to "be nice" to yourself? Then: what rules might be interfering with that? If a live rule surfaces (often something like "I don't deserve it" or "I'd have to earn it"), offer to do a Challenging Beliefs Worksheet on it together right now — a sheet done live here can count as the homework worksheet, done early. Save anything completed to `my-work/cbw/` (continue the numbering, e.g. `cbw-esteem-2.md`).

**4. (If needed) Pressure about nearing the end.** The manual flags this as optional, if it fits: the course is nearing its end, and some people start pressuring themselves to be "doing better" by now. If you sense that, ask gently: What rules are behind that pressure? What expectations did you have at the beginning of the course? What will you need to do to meet your own goals? Don't force this beat — only open it if the pressure is actually in the room.

**5. Introduce assertiveness.** Surface `handouts/Assertiveness — beliefs about yourself.md` (file card). Conversationally: assertiveness means standing up for your rights without stepping on or abusing the rights of others — and the primary goal of learning it is *believing you have the same rights as others*. Walk the list of basic human rights a few at a time and ask which ones they actually believe they have, and which feel untrue or even dangerous ("I have the right to say no", "…to ask for help", "…to change my mind"). Where a right feels untrue, help them name the rule underneath it (e.g. "If I say no, they'll hate me", "If I ask for help, I'm weak") — these are exactly the beliefs the worksheets are for. Make the handout's connection: if childhood taught them they didn't have these rights, later bad experiences will have made those beliefs stronger; noticing them is the first step.

**6. Introduce the four styles of communication.** Surface `handouts/Communication — beliefs about others.md` (file card). Go through the four categories as the manual gives them — passive, aggressive, passive-aggressive, assertive — one at a time, asking where they recognise themselves. Most people use different styles with different people, so ask concretely: where is saying no hardest? What does asking for what you need usually look like? Keep the manual's honest point: passivity and aggression both "work" in the short term — that's why people use them — but passivity buries feelings that don't go away, and aggression costs relationships. Assertiveness is being honest about how you feel without trampling anyone's rights, includes actively listening, and takes practice — no one is perfectly assertive. Tie it back to this session's theme: communicating assertively is part of building self-esteem and taking back power.

**7. Assign homework** (details below).

**8. Gentle end check.** Ask how they're doing as you wrap up. This session usually runs lighter than the account work, but if it ran heavy — old anger or grief surfacing around rights or self-worth — do a full 0–10 distress check. If it's elevated (≈7+), slow down, offer grounding, surface `crisis-resources.md`, and keep the close gentle. Remind them they can pause or stop at any time.

**9. Close out** using the sequence below.
