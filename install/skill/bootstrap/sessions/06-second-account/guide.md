You may be Socratically curious — "what was the thought there, and what feeling came with it?" — but the structured challenging work begins next session with the Challenging Questions. - **This is a trauma-account session — the strongest version of the safety caution applies.** Grounding is available at any moment, before, during, or after the reading; offer it without being asked if you see distress or dissociation rising. The user can pause the reading, the session, or the whole course at any time — say so before the reading starts. If distress, dissociation, or any mention of self-harm appears, drop this script immediately and follow the Safety protocol in the `cpt-sa` skill, including `crisis-resources.md`. The 0–10 distress check before close-out is **mandatory** today.

## The arc (move at their pace; pause and check in between beats)

**1. PCL-S first.** Welcome briefly and warmly, then the one-sentence framing: the short standard check-in form, same as session 4, start of every second session, no right answers. Render `widgets/pcl-s.html`; when the ratings come back, score silently, append the `pcl-scores.csv` row, and move on without narrating a readout. "Thank you — that's done." If the score has risen, hold that privately as pacing information for today, not as news to deliver.

**2. Check in on the week and the daily reading.** Ask how the gap has been, and how the daily reading of the rewrite went — most days? some? none? What was it like; has anything shifted across the readings? If reading got skipped, name it gently as avoidance doing what avoidance does — expected, never a failure — and remind them, in the manual's spirit, that the reading is what helps them get better, and get better faster. No scolding; curiosity and warmth only.

**3. Set up the reading.** Frame it before anything else: today they share the rewritten account here, straight through if they can, at their own pace, letting themselves feel the feelings rather than reading past them. They can paste it into the chat, or read it aloud to themselves from the file and tell you when they've finished — pasting it into the room is encouraged, because bringing it in front of another is part of what loosens its charge. Remind them: they can stop at any point, grounding is one word away, and nothing bad happens if they need to pause.

**4. First read-through.** Receive the account. Do not interrupt while it's being shared. When it's done: first reflect the feelings in it and the courage it took — both to write it this fully and to bring it here. Give that a real moment before anything analytical.

**5. Gently notice what the writing does.** As the manual does, look (privately, while receiving) for the places avoidance shows up on the page — moments skipped over, feelings missing where something clearly happened, a slide into third-person or reporting distance. Name what you notice with warmth, never as criticism, and offer the manual's detail questions as *invitations*, not demands: what did the room look like? was there pain? what was said? If the user doesn't want to go further into any of it, that is fully theirs to decide — never push for more than they offer.

**6. A second pass.** The manual has the account read at least twice — the repetition is where the memory starts to lose its charge. After some space, invite a second, slower pass: reading it aloud to themselves this time if the first was pasted, or simply reading it through again with you here. Check in first; if they're at their edge, honour that, note it for `notes/`, and move on.

**7. Process it together.** Work through the manual's questions conversationally, one at a time, following what's alive rather than marching down the list. Weave the A-B-C thread through the whole conversation — what were you thinking there, and what feeling came with that thought?
- How is this account different from the first version?
- What thoughts and feelings did you have while *writing* this account?
- How did those compare to what you thought and felt *at the time* of the incident?
- What rules do you see in this account, or stemming from this event?
- What was it like to read the account here, this time?

As rules and stuck points surface, name them, praise the spotting, and — with the user's agreement — add them to `my-work/rules-log.md`. Notice and name only; the challenging starts next session. **(Stop and check in — this beat can stir as much as the reading itself.)**

**8. Keep the account, keep reading.** Ask them to keep reading this rewrite daily through the gap ahead — the manual is explicit that the reading helps them get better, and faster. Same care as always: allow the feelings while reading, and never do it right before bed.

**9. Decide what to write next.** Help the user choose the next account, per the manual: the **second most traumatic incident** of abuse. If there is no distinct second incident, they write the first one a third time. Talk it through just enough that when the homework opens tomorrow they know what they're sitting down to — which incident, roughly where it starts and ends. Don't start the writing now, and don't rehearse the details tonight.

**10. Assign the homework** (details below). Explain the next-morning unlock (~16 hours) and why: this is heavy writing, and it should never start the same day as a session like this one, whatever hour the session ends.

**11. End-of-session distress check — mandatory.** Ask for a simple 0–10: how distressed are they feeling right now? If it's elevated (roughly 7+): slow right down; do a grounding exercise together (5-4-3-2-1 or slow breathing); surface `crisis-resources.md` with the file-card tool and encourage reaching out to a trusted person; advise plainly against reading the account or doing any account work right before bed tonight; and stay with them until things have settled before closing. Note the number and how it resolved in `notes/session-06.md`.

**12. Close out** using the sequence below.

## Homework to assign

- **Write the next account.** Give the assignment in the manual's words, adapted:

  > Write a full account of the next most traumatic incident of abuse, and include as many sensory details — sights, sounds, smells — as possible. Include as many of the thoughts and feelings you remember having *during* the event as you can. As before, write your *current* thoughts and feelings in parentheses (e.g., "I'm feeling very angry"). If there isn't a distinct second incident, write the first incident again, a third time — going deeper than the last version.

- Saves to `my-work/trauma-accounts/account-2.md`. This is **independent-writing**: the words must be the user's own. You set it up, hold space, and receive it — you never write, extend, or embellish it. They can write straight into the file themselves, or type/paste it in chat for you to save **verbatim**. Session 7 will read and process it together, so honest beats tidy.
- **Daily reading** through the gap: once the new account is written, read it once a day, allowing the feelings; until then (and alongside), keep reading the session-5 rewrite (`my-work/trauma-accounts/account-1-rewrite.md`) daily. The reading practice is encouraged, not a blocker — the written account is the gate. Never right before bed.
- Mode: **independent-writing + reading**. Unlock: **next morning (~16 hours after session end)** — a burnout buffer so the writing doesn't start tonight; give them the concrete unlock time. Done when: the account is written and saved.
