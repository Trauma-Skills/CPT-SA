# Challenging Beliefs Worksheet

This is the master worksheet for the rest of the course. It brings together everything so far — noticing the situation–thought–feeling chain (A-B-C), the Challenging Questions, and the Problematic Thinking Patterns — and then goes one step further: building an alternative thought and noticing what changes when you believe it.

The worksheet moves through eight columns. Each one is described below, followed by two worked examples.

## The columns

**A. Situation** — Describe the event, thought, or belief leading to the unpleasant emotion(s).

**B. Thought / Rule** — Write the thought or rule related to Column A. Rate your belief in each thought from 0–100% (how much do you believe this thought?).

**C. Emotion(s)** — Specify each emotion (sad, angry, etc.) and rate how strongly you feel it from 0–100%.

**D. Challenging Thoughts** — Use the Challenging Questions to examine your automatic thoughts from Column B. Is the thought balanced and factual, or extreme?

- Evidence for?
- Evidence against?
- Habit or fact?
- Interpretations not accurate?
- All or none?
- Extreme or exaggerated?
- Out of context?
- Source unreliable?
- Low versus high probability?
- Based on feelings or facts?
- Irrelevant factors?

**E. Problematic Patterns** — Use the Problematic Thinking Patterns sheet to decide whether this is one of your problematic patterns of thinking:

- Jumping to conclusions
- Exaggerating or minimizing
- Disregarding important aspects
- Oversimplifying
- Over-generalizing
- Mind reading
- Emotional reasoning

**F. Alternative Thought(s)** — What else can I say instead of Column B? How else can I interpret the event instead of Column B? Rate your belief in each alternative thought from 0–100%.

**G. Re-rate Old Thought(s)** — Re-rate how much you now believe the thought(s) in Column B, from 0–100%.

**H. Emotion(s)** — Now what do you feel? Rate each emotion from 0–100%.

## Worked example 1 — a safety belief

**A. Situation:** I have to ride on a plane.

**B. Thought(s):**
- Air travel is dangerous. — 75%
- I could be killed. — 50%

**C. Emotion(s):** Afraid — 100%. Helpless — 75%. Anxious — 75%.

**D. Challenging Thoughts:**
- *Evidence for?* People have been killed.
- *Evidence against?* Airport security has been increased.
- *Based on feelings or facts?* I am letting myself believe this because I feel scared and not because it is realistic.

**E. Problematic Patterns:**
- *Emotional reasoning:* I feel very strongly that I will be hurt or killed flying. — 95%

**F. Alternative Thought(s):**
- The chances are very small that I will be killed or hurt while flying. — 95%
- Even if the plane blew up, I could not do anything about it. — 80%

**G. Re-rate Old Thought(s):** 15%, 10%.

**H. Emotion(s):** Afraid — 40%. Helpless — 5%. Anxious — 10%.

## Worked example 2 — a belief about anger

**A. Situation:** If I express anger, I'll be out of control.

**B. Thought(s):**
- Anger is not right, so it is wrong. — 50%
- Angry people are scary. I don't want to be that way. — 90%
- If I let myself go, I'll be destructive. — 85%

**C. Emotion(s):** Angry — 50%. Fear — 95%.

**D. Challenging Thoughts:**
- *Evidence for?* Feeling my anger chokes me because I don't let it out.
- *Evidence against?* Even Jesus got angry. I have never been really destructive when I was angry.
- *Extreme or exaggerated?* My phrases and words to describe anger are exaggerated.

**E. Problematic Patterns:**
- *Exaggerating or minimizing:* Anger is rage, instead of what it is — unpleasant.
- *Emotional reasoning:* Because anger feels bad, it is bad/wrong, so I must be, too.

**F. Alternative Thought(s):**
- Anger is appropriate in some situations. — 100%
- Anger can be expressed without aggression. — 60%
- Anger is an emotion like sadness. I let myself feel that and it is not overwhelming. — 60%

**G. Re-rate Old Thought(s):** 20%, 75%, 50%.

**H. Emotion(s):** Angry — 50%. Fear — 70%.
