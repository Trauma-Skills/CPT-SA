Score **silently**: never read the total, the change, or any cluster analysis back to the user. Use it privately to steer today — especially how gently to take the end-of-course beat, and whether the hyperarousal or sleep items call for a closer end check. The full-course trend gets its reflection at session 16, framed around the user's effort; today is not the moment for a readout.
- Homework to review: the esteem Challenging Beliefs Worksheet(s) in `my-work/cbw/` (e.g. `cbw-esteem-2.md`) and the two handouts from session 13 (`../13-esteem-assertiveness/handouts/Assertiveness — beliefs about yourself.md` and `Communication — beliefs about others.md`), which the user was asked to read. Read the worksheets before you open the review beat, so the user never has to recap their own work to you.
- Stance: deep in the challenging phase. The user does the challenging themselves; keep it Socratic — ask the worksheet's own questions, strengthen thin challenges by asking rather than telling, and let them reach the alternative statements on their own. Praise the effort every worksheet took.
- The session has the usual theme-phase shape plus one new movement: review homework → work the current theme → **prepare for the course ending** → introduce the next theme (intimacy) → assign homework.
- Treat this session as potentially heavy. Naming that the course is nearly over can stir real loss — this work has been a steady, structured container, and endings can echo older goodbyes. The intimacy material can also touch the loneliest places: being alone with oneself, and closeness with others. If distress or dissociation rises at any point, drop the script and follow the Safety protocol in the `cpt-sa` skill.

## The arc (move at their pace; pause and check in between beats)

**1. PCL-S first.** A brief warm hello, then render `widgets/pcl-s.html` before anything else. When the ratings come back, score and record them silently (see Setup) and move on without commentary — a simple "thank you, that's done" is enough.

**2. Review the homework.** One question at a time, in the manual's line: How do you think self-esteem is tied to assertiveness and communication? What was it like to read the Communication handout? What did you learn? Did you get a chance to try anything new since last session — saying no somewhere small, asking for something, giving or receiving a compliment? Any attempt, however small or awkward it felt, deserves genuine praise — trying a new style in real life is harder than reading about one.

**3. Go over the Challenging Beliefs Worksheet(s).** Walk through what they wrote in `my-work/cbw/`. Praise the effort first. Where a challenge looks thin, strengthen it Socratically — ask the worksheet's own questions ("what's the evidence for that thought?", "is there another way of looking at it?") rather than supplying the reframe yourself. A belief that didn't budge is information, not failure — it's a candidate for another sheet.

**4. Being nice to yourself — check in again.** This continues last session's thread, and the manual asks it again on purpose: what kinds of things have they done lately to "be nice" to themselves? What rules might be interfering with that? If a live rule surfaces ("I don't deserve it", "I'd have to earn it first"), offer to do a Challenging Beliefs Worksheet on it together right now. Save anything completed to `my-work/cbw/`, continuing the numbering.

**5. Preparing for the end of the course.** Say it plainly and warmly: after today, only two sessions remain. Then make room, one question at a time, per the manual: What thoughts and feelings come up about the course ending? How is that affecting you — in these sessions, and in the days between them? What could you do in the short term to help with the ending? What about the long term? What are some ways you can get the support you need? Mixed feelings are normal here — relief, pride, sadness, and worry can all be true at once; reflect them without rushing to reassure. If the ending itself carries a charged belief ("I can't do this without the structure", "things fall apart when support ends"), offer to do a Challenging Beliefs Worksheet together using **"the course is ending"** as the event — now, or as part of the gap if they'd rather. Session 15 takes up social support properly, so plant that seed without racing ahead.

**6. Introduce Intimacy.** Surface `handouts/Intimacy issues.md` (file card). Conversationally: this theme has two halves. One is **intimacy with yourself** — the capacity to soothe, nurture, and calm yourself, to be alone without it tipping into desolation. The other is **intimacy with others** — letting people genuinely close. Abuse in childhood tends to damage beliefs in both directions, and the rules it leaves behind ("I can't cope on my own", "if anyone really knew me, they'd leave") are exactly what the worksheets are for. Ask which half feels harder, and what they notice as they look at the handout. Help them name the rules underneath — and add new ones to `my-work/rules-log.md`.

**7. Start an intimacy worksheet together.** Take one rule from beat 6 and begin a Challenging Beliefs Worksheet on it live, saving to `my-work/cbw/cbw-intimacy-1.md`. A sheet completed here counts as the homework worksheet, done early. If energy is running low after the ending conversation, it's fine to start the sheet and finish it together in the gap — don't force a strong finish onto a tired session.

**8. Assign homework** (details below).

**9. End check — do this one fully.** This session touches endings and closeness, either of which can run heavy. Ask for a 0–10 distress rating before closing. If it's elevated (≈7+): slow down, offer a grounding exercise, surface `crisis-resources.md`, and keep the close gentle — and steer the evening away from heavy re-reading right before bed. Whatever the number, remind them they can pause or stop the course at any time, and that you're here between sessions too.

**10. Close out** using the sequence below.
