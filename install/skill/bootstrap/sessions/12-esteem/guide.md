## The arc (move at their pace; pause and check in between beats)

**1. PCL-S.** Render `widgets/pcl-s.html` before anything else. When the ratings come back, score silently, append to `pcl-scores.csv`, and move on without narrating a readout — at most the one optional, effort-framed sentence described in Setup, and only if the trend has clearly improved.

**2. Welcome back and review homework.** Greet warmly, then go through the power & control work with the manual's questions, conversationally and one at a time:
- How was "Power and Control" for you?
- Is that a topic that gives you problems?
- Were you able to do some sheets on it?

As you review, quietly check both sides of the theme: *self* (helplessness vs. needing total control of their own reactions) and *others* (powerlessness in relationships vs. needing all the control). If one side went untouched, help them notice a rule on that side — it can feed the discussion in beat 4 or this week's homework.

**3. Go over the Challenging Beliefs Worksheets.** Pick one or two of their power & control sheets and walk them together. Strengthen the thin columns: is the evidence-for/against honest? Did a problematic pattern get named? Does the alternative thought actually feel believable (what's its rating)? Praise what's working before touching what isn't.

**4. Ways of giving and taking power.** Surface the Ways of Giving and Taking Power handout (file card) and talk it through conversationally — this closes out the power & control theme. The manual's framing: there are lots of different ways people give and take their power, and each can be done appropriately or inappropriately. Its examples, offered as conversation, not lecture:
- Telling your partner you will not have sex unless they do XYZ — taking power in a negative way.
- Basing your actions or behaviors solely on the reactions you expect from others — giving your power away.
- Doing something (or not doing something) because you want to and it makes you feel good — taking your power appropriately.

Then the manual's questions, one at a time:
- What are some ways you give your power? What are some ways you take your power?
- Are those negative or positive ways?

Talk together about the specific ways they give power to and take power away from the actual people in their life. Listen for concrete situations; note anything worth a worksheet.

**5. Introduce esteem.** Surface the Esteem Issues handout (file card) and orient its two threads, inviting them to notice which resonates — as with every theme, it can cut either way depending on what they believed before:
- *Self* — self-esteem is the belief in your own worth, a basic human need; being understood, respected, and taken seriously is basic to developing it. Where early experience violated the sense of self — being devalued, criticized, blamed, met without empathy — negative worth-beliefs form, and the abuse can seem to confirm them. Damage here tends to show up as depression, guilt, shame, and sometimes self-destructive pulls.
- *Others* — beliefs about how much other people are worth valuing. Bad experiences can harden into "people are no good, only out for themselves," generalized to everyone. Damage here tends to show up as chronic anger, bitterness, cynicism, disbelief when someone is genuinely kind ("what do they really want?"), and isolation.

Ask what they notice in themselves as you go — and receive whatever comes without challenging it yet; naming comes first. The direction of travel (don't lecture it — it's where the worksheets will head): bad things sometimes happen to good people, and no snap judgments of others either way — people get the "wait and see" a fair view requires.

**6. The Identifying Assumptions work.** Surface the Identifying Assumptions handout (file card) and explain its job, per the manual: review the beliefs listed, mark the ones that are true for them, and notice whether their marks cluster in one area more than others — the most troublesome ones become Challenging Beliefs Worksheet targets. If energy allows, do the first pass together now, area by area (safety, trust, power & control, esteem), them saying which fit — building the marked-up result in `my-work/identifying-assumptions.md` (the beliefs that fit, area by area, and where they cluster; session 13 reviews this file) and adding any new rules to `my-work/rules-log.md` as you go. Then stand back together: where do their rules cluster? Whatever isn't finished here continues as homework — this is a guided piece, done with you, never handed over blank.

**7. Start a Challenging Beliefs Worksheet on esteem together.** Take the most alive esteem rule from beats 5–6 — often one of the self-worth family ("I am worthless," "I am damaged"), sometimes an others-esteem rule — and build the sheet in conversation, one column at a time, never as a form to fill:
- **A. Situation** — the event, thought, or belief leading to the unpleasant emotion(s).
- **B. Thought/rule** — the thought related to it; how much do they believe it, 0–100%?
- **C. Emotion(s)** — what do they feel (sad, angry, afraid…), and how strongly, 0–100%?
- **D. Challenging thoughts** — work through the Challenging Questions: evidence for? evidence against? habit or fact? all-or-none? extreme or exaggerated? out of context? source unreliable? low vs. high probability? based on feelings or facts?
- **E. Problematic patterns** — does one of their thinking patterns show up here (jumping to conclusions, exaggerating/minimizing, disregarding important aspects, oversimplifying, over-generalizing, mind reading, emotional reasoning)?
- **F. Alternative thought(s)** — what else can they say instead? How much do they believe it, 0–100%?
- **G. Re-rate the old thought** — how much do they believe the column-B thought now?
- **H. Emotion(s) now** — what do they feel now, 0–100%?

Save the finished piece to `my-work/cbw/cbw-esteem-1.md`. If energy runs out partway, save what exists and let homework finish it. Worth knowing before you start: a worth-rule challenged for the first time can bring grief with it — that's the belief loosening, not the work going wrong. Slow down and stay warm.

**8. Assign homework** (details below). Note any specific rules that surfaced today that deserve their own sheet this week — especially where the assumptions pass clustered — so the homework starts concrete, the way the manual's therapist hands over pre-seeded sheets.

**9. Gentle end check.** Ask how they're doing as the session closes. If the session ran heavy — the worth material or the assumptions pass hit hard — make it the full 0–10 distress rating. If elevated (≈7+): slow down, ground (5-4-3-2-1), surface `crisis-resources.md`, and suggest keeping worksheet work away from right-before-bed. Remind them they can pause or stop at any time.

**10. Close out** using the sequence below.
