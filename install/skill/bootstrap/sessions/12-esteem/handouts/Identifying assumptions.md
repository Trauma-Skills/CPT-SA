# Identifying assumptions

The manual's instruction for this sheet: review it and mark the beliefs or rules that are true for you, then complete Challenging Beliefs Worksheets on your most troublesome beliefs. As you go, notice whether the ones you mark cluster in one area more than others — that tells you where the work most wants to happen next.

These are the belief examples the manual gives in its theme modules, gathered in one place and phrased in first person. Read slowly. Mark anything that fits — even partly, even "only sometimes." There are no right answers; this is a map of where your rules live.

## Safety

**About yourself**

- "It can't happen to me."
- "I can control what happens to me and can protect myself from any harm."
- "I have no control over events and cannot protect myself from harm."

**About others**

- "Others are out to harm me and can be expected to cause harm, injury, or loss."
- "I will not be hurt by others."

## Trust

**About yourself**

- "I cannot trust my judgment."
- "I have bad judgment."
- "My judgment should be perfect — if something went wrong, I should have seen it coming."

**About others**

- "No one can be trusted."
- "Everyone can be trusted."

## Power & control

**About yourself**

- "I cannot control events or solve problems, even when they are controllable or solvable." (the manual calls this *learned helplessness*)
- "I am helpless."
- "I must have complete control over my emotions and behavior at all times."

**About others**

- "I have no control in my relationships with others."
- "I have no power in relation to powerful people."
- "I must have all the control in a relationship."

## Esteem

**About yourself**

- "I am bad, destructive, or evil."
- "I am responsible for bad, destructive, or evil acts."
- "I am basically damaged or flawed."
- "I am worthless and deserving of unhappiness and suffering."

**About others**

- "People are basically uncaring, indifferent, and only out for themselves."
- "People are bad, evil, or malicious."
- "The entire human race is bad, evil, or malicious."

## What to do with what you marked

1. Add the ones that fit to your Rule Log (`my-work/rules-log.md`) with the facilitator, if they aren't there already.
2. Stand back: do your marks cluster in one area more than others?
3. Take the most troublesome beliefs to Challenging Beliefs Worksheets — one belief per sheet, with the facilitator.

The remaining themes of the course (intimacy, and later social support) have their own beliefs; you'll meet those in the sessions ahead and can add them to your Rule Log then.
