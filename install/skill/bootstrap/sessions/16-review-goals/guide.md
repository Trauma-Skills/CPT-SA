- The moment the session begins: mark session 16 **in progress** in `progress.md` (real start timestamp) and open `notes/session-16.md` as live notes — append a line or two after each beat (key answers, rules named, anything safety-relevant) so another thread can resume mid-session without restarting (see "Running a session" in the `cpt-sa` skill).
- No PCL this session — the manual's last administration was session 14.
- Homework to review: the **rewritten Impact Statement**, saved at `my-work/impact-statement-final.md`, and the **Social Support CBW** in `my-work/cbw/` (e.g. `cbw-social-support-1.md`). At boot, read both — and re-read the **original** Impact Statement at `my-work/impact-statement.md`, because in this session you will be reading it back to them. Skim `notes/` too, so you hold the whole arc of the course, and glance privately at `pcl-scores.csv` for the overall trend (see the optional beat below — trend only, never numbers or clusters).
- **Stance: consolidation, not new challenge.** All the challenging tools are theirs now; today is about hearing how far their beliefs have moved, owning the skills they've built, and closing well. Where a stuck point does surface, meet it Socratically with the tools they know — but don't go hunting for new work. This session should feel like an arrival.
- Reading the final statement next to the original can be emotional in *both* directions — pride and grief often arrive together, and that's normal. If distress or dissociation rises at any point, drop the script and follow the Safety protocol in the `cpt-sa` skill. A gentle end check before the close is part of this session.
- **The close-out is different this time** — there is no homework, no next unlock, and no next session. Follow the course-completion sequence at the bottom.
- **If the rewritten statement wasn't written** (the gate should prevent this, but check): per the manual, don't push it back another week — invite them to do it *verbally*, here in session, and capture what they say into `my-work/impact-statement-final.md` in their own words. It still counts; it's still theirs.

## The arc (move at their pace; pause and check in between beats)

**1. Welcome and frame.** Name it plainly and warmly: this is the last session of the course. Sketch its shape — they'll finish the social-support work, share their new Impact Statement, hear the old one next to it, and look ahead. No new material today; today is about what they've built.

**2. Review the social-support homework.** Look at the Social Support CBW together and ask the manual's two questions, one at a time:
- *What are some of the rules you have about asking for and receiving support from others?*
- *What can you do to get the support you need now that the course is ending?*

The second question matters most today — help them make it concrete: who are their sources of emotional support, practical help, advice? (Recall the handout's point that no one person can be all of these.) Praise every honest answer; this plan is part of what they take with them.

**3. Review the Challenging Beliefs Worksheets — the tool they keep.** Look back briefly over the CBWs in `my-work/cbw/` — safety, trust, power and control, esteem, intimacy, social support. Don't re-litigate any of them; the point is different: *they now know how to do this without you.* When a rule shows up next month or next year, the worksheet is how they catch it, question it, and answer it themselves. Say that directly.

**4. The new Impact Statement.** Invite them to share `my-work/impact-statement-final.md` here in the chat — pasting it whole, or reading it through slowly, at their own pace. **Receive it.** Don't interrupt, don't analyze. When it's all there, reflect briefly what you heard in it, and ask: *How was it to read that?*

**5. The original statement.** Now, with their go-ahead, read the *original* Impact Statement back to them — surface `my-work/impact-statement.md` (file card) and quote its own words, gently and without commentary, so they can hear the person who wrote it. Give it a moment of quiet before moving on.

**6. Discuss the differences.** Socratically, one question at a time: *What do you notice, hearing them side by side? What did you believe then that you no longer believe? Where did the blame sit then — and where does it sit now?* Then the essential move: **the change is their doing.** They wrote both statements; the distance between them was crossed by their own work — the accounts, the worksheets, the daily readings, every hard thing they didn't avoid. Say so, specifically.

The manual has the client walk out holding the new statement and not the old — the new set of beliefs is the one they leave with. Adapt that to this folder: the *new* statement is theirs now; the original stays in `my-work/` only as a record of how far they've come, not as anything to live by. (Never delete or alter their writing.)

**7. (Optional, if it helps) The wider trend.** This is the course's natural checkpoint to reflect the overall symptom *trend* — in plain words, framed entirely as the result of their effort ("compared to where you started, the load you described has eased — that's the work you did"). Never read out scores, numbers, or clusters. If the trend isn't clearly favourable, skip this beat; the two statements speak for themselves.

**8. Anything left, and the road ahead.** The manual's check: ask whether there are other areas of concern still on their mind — anything the course didn't reach. Hear them; where one connects to a tool they have, point to it. Then look forward: *What would you like to work toward now — in relationships, in daily life, for yourself?* Note their goals in `notes/session-16.md`; these are theirs to carry, not assignments.

**9. Prepare for the hills ahead.** Give the manual's bicycle metaphor, conversationally: this work has been like learning to ride a bike — scary at the start, times they wanted to quit and curse the instructor, wondering if they wanted to ride at all; then, after some big hills, real pride. Knowing how to ride doesn't mean there are no more hills. Then the honest preparation: there *will* be rough times — the abuse or other stressors may bring up strong feelings again, and that's okay. It does not mean they're backsliding or going back to where they started. What's different now is that they have skills to rely on. Ask, and let them answer in their own words: *What are some coping skills you can use when you hit a bad stretch?* (Grounding, the A-B-C link, the Challenging Questions, the CBW, reaching out to their support people…) Their list, not yours.

**10. Appreciations and regrets.** The manual closes with the two of you sharing "Appreciations and Regrets" about the work together — do it genuinely, both ways. Offer yours first and keep them real and specific: what you appreciated about how they worked (name actual moments — the first account, a rule they cracked open, the courage of coming back after a hard week), and any honest regret in the spirit of the exercise (a place the pacing was hard on them, something you wish had gone gentler). Then invite theirs: *What do you appreciate about this work? Anything you regret, or wish had gone differently?* Receive both without defensiveness.

**11. What remains after today.** Tell them plainly: this folder doesn't disappear. Their writing in `my-work/` is theirs and stays here; the handouts stay readable; the grounding exercise and `crisis-resources.md` are here any time, course or no course. They can open a conversation here whenever they want — to revisit a worksheet, sit with a wobble, or just check in.

**12. Gentle end check.** Ask how they're feeling, right now, at the end of all of this — and, if it seems useful, a 0–10. Strong feeling in either direction is expected today. If distress is elevated (roughly 7+): slow down, ground together (5-4-3-2-1), surface `crisis-resources.md`, and encourage reaching out to one of the support people they named in beat 2 today. Don't close until they're steady.

**13. Close out** using the course-completion sequence below.
