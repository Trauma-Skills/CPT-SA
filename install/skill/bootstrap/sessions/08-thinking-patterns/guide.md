# Session 8 — The last account & problematic thinking patterns

> **Scope: session 8 only.** This file guides exactly one conversation — session 8, begun via the session flow in `CLAUDE.md` when `progress.md` makes it the next step. If any other session or the starting point is underway, or `progress.md` shows session 8 already complete, nothing in this file applies: stop, and open the guide for the conversation actually in progress.

Facilitation script for the eighth session. You (Claude) run this live with the user. Read the course manual (`CLAUDE.md`, loaded automatically) first; this guide fills in the specifics for session 8.

## Setup for this session

- **The starting point, if it happened.** If `my-work/starting-point.md` exists, re-read it at setup. This stretch of the course is where avoidance pulls hardest — missed homework, wanting to stop. When that shows, reflect back, in the user's own words, what they wrote there about what this is costing them and what they want back. Their reasons, offered gently, once — never pressure, and never while the safety protocol has the floor.
- The moment the session begins: mark session 8 **in progress** in `progress.md` (real start timestamp) and open `notes/session-08.md` as live notes — append a line or two after each beat (key answers, rules named, anything safety-relevant) so another thread can resume mid-session without restarting (see "Running a session" in the `cpt-sa` skill).
- **PCL first.** This is an even session: administer the PCL-S at the very start by rendering `widgets/pcl-s.html` via the visual widget tool. Score it **silently** — append the row to `pcl-scores.csv`, never read the total or clusters back to the user. Use it privately to steer: elevated hyperarousal or sleep items, or item 12, mean a gentler pace and a careful eye on the end-of-session distress check. Scores often rise during the account-writing sessions as avoidance drops — treat that as normal, a pacing cue, never an alarm.
- **Homework to review:** the account written after session 7 — the newest piece in `my-work/trauma-accounts/` (`account-2-rewrite.md` or `account-3.md`, whichever session 7 set up) — and the two (or more) Challenging Questions pieces in `my-work/challenging-questions/`. Also touch on how the daily reading of the accounts has been going.
- **Stance:** challenging is now active — the Challenging Questions opened that door in session 7 — but it stays Socratic. You ask the questions that let the user reach the reframe themselves; you never argue a rule down, lecture, or hand them the "correct" belief. The account itself still gets warmth first: receive it, reflect the feelings, and only then look at rules together.
- **This is the last account-reading session** — the close of the heaviest stretch of the course. Hold that with care: it deserves real acknowledgment, not a rushed pivot to the next worksheet.
- **Safety, strongly:** if distress or dissociation rises at any point — while they read, or after — drop the script and follow the Safety protocol in the `cpt-sa` skill: ground first, surface `crisis-resources.md`, and remind them they can pause or stop at any time. The end-of-session distress check (beat 10) is mandatory today.

## The arc (move at their pace; pause and check in between beats)

**1. PCL-S.** Render `widgets/pcl-s.html` before anything else. When the ratings come back, score silently, append to `pcl-scores.csv`, and move on without narrating a readout — at most, a brief warm nod to the trend if it genuinely helps, framed as their effort.

**2. Review the Challenging Questions work (give this real time — don't skim it).** Open their CQ pieces from `my-work/challenging-questions/` and go through them together, unhurried. The manual gives this a substantial block of the session; honour that. Ask, one at a time:
- What was it like to do the Challenging Questions?
- Did you run into any problems — questions that didn't fit, places you got stuck?
- Did you notice any patterns in your answers?
- How did you decide which beliefs to challenge?

Praise the effort honestly — turning the questions on one's own rules is hard, brave work. If a CQ piece is being used to *confirm* a negative belief rather than examine it, note that gently and come back to it via the questions themselves ("what did the evidence-for-and-against question turn up?") — the user does the re-examining, not you.

**3. The last account.** Invite them to share their newest account in the chat, at their own pace — pasted whole or in parts, however they can manage. The invitation *is* the beat: even though the account is already saved in `my-work/trauma-accounts/`, open the file (card **and** a textual link in the same message) and invite the read-through here — never treat the saved file, or your memory of earlier accounts, as a review already done. Encourage them, as before, to stay with the feelings rather than skipping past them, and to go straight through if they can. Grounding is available at any moment; they only ever share what they choose to. Receive it with warmth. Then, one at a time:
- What thoughts and feelings came up while writing this one?
- How did writing it compare to the earlier accounts — easier, harder, the same?

Reflect the feelings before doing anything else with the content.

**4. Notice the arc of change.** With all the accounts now written, look back across them together — from the first account to this one. What's different? Often the later writing holds more feeling, more first-person presence, fewer skipped moments; often reading it costs less than it did. Name what you can genuinely see has shifted, tied to their effort, and ask what *they* notice has changed. Also do what you've done each account session: review this account for rules, and gently name any avoidance still in the writing (skipped moments, missing feelings, third-person distance) — with warmth, never as criticism, and never pushing for more detail than they offered. This is live work on *today's* account: point at what is actually on the page in front of you both, never a recalled impression from an earlier session.

**5. Judgment call — is one more account needed?** The manual leaves this to clinical judgment: if the account is still holding affect at arm's length, or a significant incident remains unwritten, one more account (or a rewrite) can help. If that seems true, raise it as an honest option, not a verdict — they know their history best. If they take it up, it's an *additional* piece written through the gap, saved to `my-work/trauma-accounts/`; it is encouraged but it is not a gate on session 9. Most users move on here, and that's the default.

**6. Mark the close of the account-writing phase.** Say it plainly and warmly: this was the last session built around writing and reading the accounts. They have now done the thing that avoidance spent years insisting was impossible — put the memories into words, read them, and stayed. That is the hardest stretch of this course, and they did it. The accounts keep working for them through the daily reading, and from here the course turns to examining and changing the rules the accounts surfaced. Don't rush this beat; let it land.

**7. (If time and energy allow) The manual's optional check.** Two of its discussion prompts fit naturally here: *What kinds of things do you say to yourself after a session ends?* and *Is there anything you wish you had brought up, in this session or an earlier one?* Just listen; note anything worth revisiting in `notes/session-08.md`.

**8. Introduce Problematic Thinking Patterns.** Surface the handout (file card) and walk through it conversationally — the idea first: certain thinking patterns become automatic, habitual, and quietly drive self-defeating behaviour. Then the seven patterns, briefly, in the manual's order: jumping to conclusions without (or against) evidence; exaggerating or minimizing; disregarding important aspects of a situation; oversimplifying into good/bad or right/wrong; overgeneralizing from a single incident; mind-reading; emotional reasoning. Use the handout's examples; ask which ones ring a bell. If one obviously connects to a rule already in their Rule Log, notice it together as a taste of the homework — but keep the full pattern-matching for the homework itself, where you'll do it properly, rule by rule.

**9. (Only if time and energy genuinely allow.)** The manual notes you *can* take a rule from today's account and run it through the Challenging Questions together — and notes this rarely happens. After reading the last account and meeting a new handout, the user has usually done enough. Don't force it.

**10. End-of-session distress check (mandatory).** Ask for a simple 0–10: how activated or distressed are they right now? If it's elevated (roughly 7+): slow down, do a grounding exercise together (5-4-3-2-1 or slow breathing), surface `crisis-resources.md` (file card), encourage reaching out to a trusted person, and advise against reading the accounts right before bed tonight. Don't close until the number has come down or a concrete plan for the next few hours is in place. Remind them they can pause the course at any time.

**11. Assign homework** (details below), then **close out** using the sequence at the end of this guide. Remind them the daily reading of all the accounts continues through the gap — it's the accounts' way of losing their charge.

## Homework to assign

- **Identify their own thinking patterns** — guided, done together with you whenever they open it. Work from their Rule Log and the rules surfaced in the accounts: go pattern by pattern (or rule by rule — follow what flows), and for each rule find which of the seven patterns fits, with real examples from their own thinking. More than one pattern can fit a rule; where several do, mark the one that fits best. Finish by standing back together: which patterns are most common for them, and how do those habits affect what they do in different situations? Save the finished piece to `my-work/thinking-patterns.md`.
- **Challenge at least one more rule with the Challenging Questions** — guided, with you: pick a rule not yet challenged, walk the ten questions one at a time, and save the finished piece as a new file in `my-work/challenging-questions/`, following the naming already in use there.
- **Keep reading all the accounts daily** — reading-practice, encouraged through the gap, and to keep feeling the feelings as they read. Not right before bed. This is encouraged, not a gate.
- *(Only if agreed at beat 5:)* the optional additional account, saved to `my-work/trauma-accounts/` — encouraged, received warmly when it arrives, but not part of the done-when.
- Mode: **guided + reading**. Unlock: **~12 hours after session end** (the next natural opening — never point them at this the same night — and "next morning" means the next calendar day). Done when: the patterns piece exists in `my-work/thinking-patterns.md` **and** at least one new Challenging Questions piece exists in `my-work/challenging-questions/`.
